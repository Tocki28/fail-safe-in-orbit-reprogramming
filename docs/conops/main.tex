\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Project Description}

\subsubsection{Background}
The System exists to meet one need: \emph{a controller that nobody can reach
must stay operable, observable and correctable for its whole life}
(see Section~\ref{sec:ngo}).

A controller in space must be updated, watched and commanded from the ground,
with nobody able to reach it \tbr{situation}. A failed update can leave it
unable to run any firmware or accept new firmware. Without physical access that
state is permanent. It is the worst outcome this System guards against.

Rationale: the loss has happened. NASA records a mission terminated after an
in-flight software upload omitted a patch for a defective countdown timer; its
recommendations include test-bed verification of in-flight software updates
[4, Lesson 0559, p.~199].

\subsubsection{Assumptions and Constraints}
\begin{description}
\item[C-1] The ground has no direct link to the controller, only through the
  onboard computer.
\item[C-2] All software follows critical-software practice. Rigour per part is
  set later by its software class.
\item[C-3] One engineer. Demonstration date \tbd{date}.
\item[C-4] Bench hardware stands in for flight hardware \tbr{situation}.
\item[A-1] No physical access after deployment \tbr{situation}.
\item[A-2] Controller power can be lost at any instant, including during a
  firmware write \tbr{power}.
\item[A-3] The ground link exists only during contact windows \tbd{window}.
\item[A-4] One operator \tbr{operator}.
\item[A-5] Single-bit memory errors occur \tbr{situation}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Overview of the Envisioned System}

\subsubsection{Overview}
The System has two segments, far apart. The ground segment is on Earth: the
operator and the ground station. The space segment is in orbit: the onboard
computer and the controller, out of physical reach \tbr{situation}. The only
link between the two is radio (I-1). It exists only while the ground station can
see the spacecraft, during a contact window \tbd{window}. Between contacts the
space segment works alone. Inside the spacecraft, the onboard computer reaches
the controller over a redundant internal link (I-2) and can rewrite it through
the recovery path (I-3). Figure~\ref{fig:segments} shows the layout.

contacts, firmware update of the controller. Out of scope: the onboard

\section{Description of Envisioned System}

\subsection{Needs, Goals and Objectives}
\label{sec:ngo}
\begin{description}
\item[NEED] A controller that nobody can reach must stay operable, observable
  and correctable for its whole life. The three properties are three areas a
  space software specification must cover: operation, observability, and
  maintenance including in-flight modification over the specified lifetime
  [3, Annex~B, items 5.11--5.13, p.~113].
\item[G-5] Operable: the controller keeps doing its job and acts on the
  operator's commands. Capabilities JOB, CMD, FM.
\item[G-6] Observable: the ground can know what the System is doing, how
  healthy it is, and what happened between contacts. Capabilities TM.
\item[G-7] Correctable: the controller's firmware can be replaced for its whole
  life without losing the controller. Capabilities UPD.
\item[G-1] No update, and no failure during one, leaves the controller without
  firmware it can run.
\item[G-2] The System acts only on firmware and commands from the operator.
\item[G-3] Losing one connection of the internal link stops nothing.
\item[G-4] Between contacts the System handles faults alone and reports them
  later \tbr{faults}.
\item[O-1] Every scenario in Section~6.0 is demonstrated on hardware and
  recorded.
\item[N-1] Retired during drafting; now G-7. NASA requires a single need statement
  [1, \S4.1, p.~49].
\item[N-2] Retired during drafting; now G-6.
\item[N-3] Retired during drafting; now G-5.
\end{description}

\subsection{Overview of System and Key Elements}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.6cm]
\item[Ground] Operator releases firmware, sends firmware and commands, reads
  reports.
\item[Onboard computer] Checks and passes on firmware and commands, stores
  reports, watches and restores the controller.
\item[Controller] Does its job, reports, keeps known-good firmware to fall back
  on.
\end{description}

\subsection{Interfaces}
\begin{description}
\item[I-1] Ground link. Radio, contact windows only.
\item[I-2] Internal link. Redundant: losing one connection does not cut the
  controller off.
\item[I-3] Recovery path. Rewrites and restarts the controller with no
  controller firmware running.
\end{description}

\subsection{Modes of Operations}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=3cm]
\item[OPERATE] Runs confirmed firmware. New firmware can arrive \tbr{receive}.
\item[TRIAL] Runs new firmware not yet judged good \tbd{trialtime}.
\item[FALLBACK] Runs previous or last-resort firmware after a failed trial or
  start.
\item[RECOVERY] Runs nothing. Restored through I-3.
\end{description}

\subsection{Proposed Capabilities}
\begin{description}
\item[JOB-1] Do the controller's job \tbd{job}.
\item[TM-1] Send regular health reports: alive, firmware, uptime, last restart
  reason, temperature \tbr{telemetry}.
\item[TM-2] Report important events at once \tbr{telemetry}.
\item[TM-3] Timestamp every report \tbr{telemetry}.
\item[TM-4] Store reports between contacts, send all at the next one, report any
  lost.
\item[TM-5] Report the health of each internal connection \tbr{telemetry}.
\item[CMD-1] Accept commands only from the operator. Refuse replayed commands.
\item[CMD-2] Answer every command: received, refused with reason, or done with
  result.
\item[CMD-3] Refuse commands not allowed in the current state.
\item[CMD-4] Run a resent command at most once.
\item[CMD-5] Dangerous commands need two steps: arm, then execute.
\item[CMD-6] Refuse impossible values.
\item[CMD-7] Let the operator cancel an update, leaving known-good firmware
  running.
\item[CMD-8] Run commands at a set time. Refuse expired commands.
\item[CMD-9] Log every command and its answer.
\item[FM-1] Notice a silent controller and try to bring it back \tbr{faults}.
\item[FM-2] Carry on over the other connection when one dies \tbr{faults}.
\item[FM-3] Restart crashed onboard programs and continue \tbr{faults}.
\item[FM-4] Record every fault and action for the ground \tbr{faults}.
\item[UPD-1] Take firmware from the ground through dropping links.
\item[UPD-2] Refuse firmware that is damaged, not from the operator, for
  something else, or below the minimum version.
\item[UPD-3] Get the firmware to the controller.
\item[UPD-4] Switch the controller to it \tbd{switch}.
\item[UPD-5] Decide whether it is good \tbd{confirm}.
\item[UPD-6] Go back to working firmware if not, with nobody's help.
\item[UPD-7] Tell the ground what happened.
\item[UPD-8] Never leave the controller without runnable firmware.
\item[VER-1] Degrade the ground link on purpose (test only).
\item[VER-2] Cut controller power and break one connection on command (test
  only) \tbr{faultinject}.
\end{description}

\section{Support Environment}
Firmware is built and released on the ground and sent up during contacts, one
transfer across several if needed. The onboard computer keeps the last
known-good firmware. Update rate \tbd{frequency}. Nothing is repaired after
deployment \tbr{situation}.

\section{Operational Scenarios}

\subsection{Nominal Conditions}
\begin{description}
\item[OPS-100] Update: release, upload over one or more contacts, check,
  transfer \tbr{receive}, switch \tbd{switch}, judged good \tbd{confirm},
  confirmed, outcome reported.
\item[OPS-110] Telemetry: reports stamped and stored between contacts, all sent
  at the next one.
\item[OPS-120] Scheduled command: received during a contact, runs at its time,
  answer sent at the next contact.
\end{description}

\subsection{Off-Nominal Conditions}
Trigger, then expected outcome. Sources in brackets are items of [2, App.~A,
Table~2].
\begin{description}
\item[OPS-200] Ground link lost mid-transfer: resumes next contact where it
  stopped.
\item[OPS-210] Command not from operator or replayed: refused, logged [2, p.~62].
\item[OPS-220] Answer lost, command resent: not run again, ``already done''.
\item[OPS-230] Command wrong for current state: refused with reason [2, p.~63].
\item[OPS-240] Impossible value: refused with reason [2, p.~62].
\item[OPS-250] Dangerous command not armed: refused with reason.
\item[OPS-260] Command expired: refused with reason [2, p.~63].
\item[OPS-270] Report storage full: ground told how many were lost.
\item[OPS-300] Firmware damaged in transfer: refused before the controller
  [2, p.~63].
\item[OPS-310] Firmware not from operator: refused.
\item[OPS-320] Firmware below minimum version: refused.
\item[OPS-330] Firmware for another controller: refused [2, p.~63].
\item[OPS-340] Power lost during firmware write: previous firmware runs; retry
  possible [2, p.~63].
\item[OPS-350] New firmware hangs: back to previous, unaided.
\item[OPS-360] New firmware restart loop: back to previous after \tbd{bootcount}
  starts [2, p.~64].
\item[OPS-370] New firmware never judged good: back to previous.
\item[OPS-380] Previous firmware also bad: last-resort firmware runs.
\item[OPS-390] Operator cancels update: known-good firmware left running.
\item[OPS-400] Bit error in stored firmware: detected, not run \tbr{bitflip}
  [2, p.~63].
\item[OPS-410] No firmware can run: restored through I-3 [2, p.~63].
\item[OPS-500] One internal connection fails: everything continues on the
  other; reported.
\item[OPS-510] Whole internal link fails: controller keeps working; loss
  reported; I-3 still usable \tbr{bothlinks}.
\item[OPS-520] Controller silent: noticed, recovery tried, recorded
  \tbr{faults}.
\item[OPS-530] Onboard program crash: it restarts and resumes any update; the
  controller is unaffected \tbr{faults}.
\end{description}

\section{Risks and Potential Issues}
\begin{description}
\item[R-1] Both internal connections may share software; one defect could cut
  both.
\item[R-2] Controller memory may not hold last-resort plus two working firmwares.
\item[R-3] Second internal connection and recovery path not yet shown on bench.
\item[R-4] Demonstration date \tbd{date}; undemonstrated scenarios are declared,
  not dropped.
\end{description}

\section{Glossary}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.2cm]
\item[Confirmed] Firmware judged good after its trial; only confirmed firmware
  is known-good.
\item[Last-resort] Known-good firmware that updates never replace (golden
  image).
\item[Switch] The controller stops running old firmware and starts the new.
\item[Arm] First step of a dangerous command; changes nothing alone.
\item[Expiry] Time after which a waiting command is refused.
\end{description}
