\section{Purpose and Scope}
This document is the preliminary hazard analysis of the System: its hazards,
their causes and severity, and the controls proposed against them.

The hazard analysis is delivered once per safety phase, each closed by a
safety review [2,~Secs.~3.1 and 4]:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.6cm]
\item[Phase 0] Concept complete: the top hazards and their causes.
\item[Phase 1] Requirements nearly complete: all known hazards, with their
  causes, controls and risk.
\item[Phase 2] Design mostly complete: the approved controls, and how each is
  verified.
\item[Phase 3] Testing complete: proof that the verifications passed.
\end{description}
This document is the first delivery: phases 0 and 1, which small projects
combine [2,~Sec.~4]. The controls are proposals that the requirements turn
into rules; each already names its verification method
[7,~App.~F.3, p.~348].

The document also decides which software is safety-critical, which the
Software Classification left open [6,~Sec.~4.0]. It covers the software items
of that classification [6,~Sec.~3.0] in operation: all controller modes, during
and between contacts. Bench and build tools are covered where they can reach
operation.

\section{Ground Rules}
These rules are set before the analysis: what counts as a failure, which
failures are included and the fault tolerance required [3,~Sec.~3.1], and how
failures are modelled and named [4,~pp.~23--24].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hazard:} a state with the potential to cause harm [1,~p.~13].
  Here, harm is losing what the need asks for: a controller that runs, and
  that the operator can command, observe and correct.
\item \textbf{Boundary:} the radio equipment and the spacecraft power supply
  are outside the System [5,~Sec.~1.2.2]. Their failures enter as causes and
  are not analysed further.
\item \textbf{Failures included} [1,~App.~A, Table~2, pp.~62--68]:
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.0cm, labelwidth=1.8cm, labelsep=0.2cm, align=left]
  \item[\textit{Software}] a hang, crash, overflow or wrong state in any of the
    three software items;
  \item[\textit{Data}] a message corrupted, lost, repeated or late on any link;
    a full buffer;
  \item[\textit{Memory}] a write cut short by power loss; a bit flipped by
    radiation (A-5); a stored value that wrongly survives a restart;
  \item[\textit{Timing}] a watchdog reset; a restart loop; a wrong or reset
    onboard clock;
  \item[\textit{Power}] lost at any moment (A-2);
  \item[\textit{Hardware}] a failed data-link connection or reprogramming link;
  \item[\textit{Operator}] a wrong or mistimed command (A-4);
  \item[\textit{Security}] the means of proving origin disclosed.
  \end{itemize}
  Physical tampering is excluded: nobody can reach the spacecraft (A-1).
\item \textbf{Controls:} ``means of reducing the risk of exposure to a hazard''
  [1,~p.~13]: a check, a backup, an automatic response or an operator
  procedure. A control with a scenario number comes from the ConOps; one
  without is new.
\item \textbf{Operator errors:} each counts as a wrong or mistimed command.
\item \textbf{Counting failures:} one cause that disables several controls at
  once counts as one failure.
\item \textbf{Severity:} how bad the outcome is if the hazard happens
  \tbr{severity}. Controls do not change it: they make a hazard less likely,
  not less bad.
\item \textbf{Fault tolerance:} ``the ability to perform a function in the
  presence of any of a specified number of coincident, independent failure
  causes of specified types'' [9,~p.~18]. The types are the failures included
  above. The number depends on severity: a catastrophic hazard must need at
  least three failures together, a critical hazard at least two [3,~Sec.~3.1]
  \tbr{credible}. Each hazard is checked by finding the smallest set of
  failures that causes it.
\item \textbf{Likelihood:} not rated; it depends on the design
  \tbd{likelihood}.
\item \textbf{Safety effort:} full [7,~Sec.~3.2.1, p.~44], chosen by the author
  on 10 October 2026. A moderate effort suits hazards that operators have time
  to answer [7,~Sec.~3.2.2, p.~44]; between contacts, no operator can (A-3). At
  this stage, the full effort makes a fault tree mandatory and a failure modes
  analysis highly recommended [7,~Table~6-3, p.~107].
\item \textbf{Naming:} hazards H-1 to H-17 in order of severity; fault-tree
  gates G01 to G05 and primary events B01 to B10. A number is never reused.
\item \textbf{Assumptions:} those of the ConOps, A-1 to A-5; this analysis
  adds none. Numbers of objectives (O), assumptions (A), constraints (C), risks
  (R), capabilities and scenarios refer to the ConOps
  [5,~Secs.~1.1.2, 3.1, 3.5, 6.0 and 8.0].
\item \textbf{Software role} [2,~Sec.~3.2.2]: CAUSE, a software fault can lead
  to the hazard; CONTROL, software prevents it; MITIGATION, software limits its
  effect; MONITOR, software detects and reports it.
\item \textbf{Verification method} [8,~Sec.~5.3.1.2.1, p.~92]:
  \textsf{\small TEST}, using the product to obtain detailed data;
  \textsf{\small DEMONSTRATION}, using it to show the control works, without
  detailed data; \textsf{\small INSPECTION}, examining the product or its
  records; \textsf{\small ANALYSIS}, modelling or calculation.
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Legend: severity}, as defined in [7,~Table~2-2, p.~27], then
for the System
\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.9cm]
\item[CATASTROPHIC] ``Loss of human life or permanent disability; loss of
  entire system; loss of ground facility; severe environmental damage''. The
  controller is lost for the rest of the mission: it cannot run, or can no
  longer be commanded, observed or corrected.
\item[CRITICAL] ``Severe injury or temporary disability; major system or
  environmental damage''. The controller acts against the operator's intent,
  or cannot be commanded, observed or corrected until the operator acts at a
  later contact.
\item[MODERATE] ``Minor injury; minor system damage''. Part of a function is
  lost, such as some health reports or one update attempt; the controller
  still runs and obeys the operator.
\item[NEGLIGIBLE] ``No injury or minor injury; some system stress, but no
  system damage''. Nothing is lost; the operator has extra work or gets data a
  contact later.
\end{description}

\section{Common Causes}
A common cause defeats several controls at once and can negate a redundancy
[4,~Sec.~1.3, p.~3], including the same defect in software that runs in more
than one place [1,~App.~A.1.1, p.~62].

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Wrong onboard clock:} expiry, arm time-outs, scheduled
commands, time-tags and the trial time limit depend on it [1,~p.~65]. H-4,
H-6, H-9, H-11, H-13.\par
\noindent\textbf{Shared image code:} start-up code, or a defect common to the
current, previous and last-resort firmware, stops all of them. H-1, H-8.\par
\noindent\textbf{Data kept over restarts:} a wrong stored setting, counter or
flag can stop every image or keep a wrong state [1,~p.~64]. H-1, H-6,
H-13.\par
\noindent\textbf{One power supply:} it feeds both the onboard computer and the
controller [5,~Sec.~3.3]; one loss stops the controller and the computer that
would restore it. H-1, H-8, H-10, H-11, H-15.\par
\noindent\textbf{Same data-link software:} one defect at both ends cuts both
connections (R-1) [1,~p.~66]. H-4.\par
\noindent\textbf{One proof of origin:} if one means proves both commands and
firmware, its disclosure opens both. H-2, H-5.\par
\noindent\textbf{Radiation:} bit errors strike stored firmware, running
memory, stored reports, held commands and the arm state (A-5) [1,~p.~63].
H-1, H-6, H-7, H-8, H-9, H-10.\par
\noindent\textbf{One set of build tools:} a tool defect enters every image.
H-1, H-7, H-12.\par
\noindent\textbf{Fixed onboard computer software:} it is not updated during
the mission [5,~Sec.~5.0], so its defects stay. H-1, H-3, H-4.\par

Reports cover the catastrophic and critical hazards, the two categories of
the hazard report form [7,~Fig.~F-1, p.~351]; the others stay in the
preliminary hazard list. Each report gives the conditions, worst effect,
causes, proposed controls, how the controls fail, and their common causes
[2,~Sec.~4].

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- H-1
\hazard{H-1}{The controller is left with no firmware it can run, and cannot be restored.}{CATASTROPHIC}{CAUSE, CONTROL}
\field{When} An update, any restart, or a needed restore.
\fieldin{Effect} The controller is lost for the rest of the mission (A-1).
\field{Causes} (a)~power lost while firmware is written (Scenario-600)
[1,~p.~63]; (b)~a bit error in stored firmware (Scenario-610) [1,~p.~63];
(c)~an update writes over the previous or last-resort firmware [1,~p.~65];
(d)~new and previous firmware both fail (Scenario-460); (e)~the last-resort
firmware has changed unnoticed [1,~p.~63]; (f)~a restart loop never reaches
the fall-back [1,~p.~64]; (g)~the start-up code fails or is damaged
[1,~p.~63]; (h)~the reprogramming link fails, or the onboard computer misses
the need to restore (Scenario-520); (i)~the onboard computer's known-good copy
is damaged [1,~p.~63].
\field{Controls} (1)~an update never writes over the previous or last-resort
firmware (Scenario-600)\ver{TEST}; (2)~every image is checked before it runs
(Scenario-610)\ver{TEST}; (3)~after a set number of failed starts, the
controller returns to its previous firmware (Scenario-440)\ver{TEST}; (4)~a
last-resort firmware that updates never replace (Scenario-460)\ver{TEST};
(5)~a restore through the reprogramming link, independent of the controller's
software (Scenario-480)\ver{TEST}; (6)~stored firmware and the reprogramming
link are checked between uses\ver{TEST}.
\field{Fails if} The image check passes damaged firmware; the failed-start
count is lost at a restart; the last-resort firmware, or the image the restore
puts back, shares the defect.
\fieldin{Common causes} Shared image code; data kept over restarts; one power
supply; radiation; one set of build tools; fixed onboard computer software.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- H-2
\hazard{H-2}{Firmware from someone other than the operator runs on the controller.}{CATASTROPHIC}{CONTROL}
\field{When} An update or a restore.
\fieldin{Effect} Hostile firmware runs the controller and can refuse updates
and report a false state. The restore starts only when no firmware can run
(Scenario-480), so this firmware is never removed.
\field{Causes} (a)~firmware not from the operator arrives (Scenario-410)
[1,~p.~67]; (b)~the means of proving origin is disclosed; (c)~a restore starts
a stored copy someone else replaced.
\field{Controls} (1)~the onboard computer refuses firmware not released by the
operator (Scenario-410)\ver{TEST}; (2)~the controller checks the origin again
before it runs the firmware\ver{TEST}; (3)~the operator can order a restore
while firmware runs\ver{DEMONSTRATION}.
\field{Fails if} The check accepts what it should refuse; the means of proof
is disclosed.
\fieldin{Common causes} One proof of origin.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- H-3
\hazard{H-3}{New firmware can no longer reach the controller.}{CATASTROPHIC}{CAUSE, CONTROL}
\field{When} After an update is confirmed, or after a fault in the onboard
computer.
\fieldin{Effect} The controller can no longer be corrected for the rest of
the mission.
\field{Causes} (a)~confirmed firmware runs, but cannot receive firmware;
(b)~a defect in the onboard computer's update path; (c)~a wrong stored minimum
version or controller identity refuses all firmware [1,~p.~67]; (d)~firmware
storage on the onboard computer stays full [1,~p.~66].
\field{Controls} (1)~new firmware runs a trial before it is confirmed
(Scenario-430 to Scenario-450)\ver{TEST}; (2)~it is judged good only after it
has received firmware itself\ver{TEST}; (3)~the last-resort firmware can
always receive firmware\ver{TEST}; (4)~the operator can order a return to the
last-resort firmware\ver{DEMONSTRATION}.
\field{Fails if} The trial skips the update path; the defect is in the
onboard computer, out of every control's reach.
\fieldin{Common causes} Fixed onboard computer software; data kept over
restarts.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- H-4
\hazard{H-4}{The controller can no longer be commanded or observed.}{CATASTROPHIC}{CAUSE, CONTROL}
\field{When} At any time.
\fieldin{Effect} The controller runs on its current firmware, but nobody can
command or observe it for the rest of the mission.
\field{Causes} (a)~both data-link connections fail (Scenario-510); (b)~one
software defect cuts both (R-1) [1,~p.~66]; (c)~a controller that never stops
sending blocks the data link [1,~p.~63]; (d)~a wrong onboard clock makes every
command expire, including the clock correction [1,~p.~65]; (e)~the ground
loses the means of proving origin; (f)~a false fault response marks both
connections failed (H-13).
\field{Controls} (1)~two separate data-link connections, one enough (O-3,
Scenario-500)\ver{TEST}; (2)~a lost connection is reported
(Scenario-510)\ver{TEST}; (3)~the reprogramming link stays usable
(Scenario-510)\ver{TEST}; (4)~the clock correction does not depend on the
onboard clock\ver{TEST}.
\field{Fails if} One cause cuts both connections. No control yet covers a
lost means of proving origin.
\fieldin{Common causes} Same data-link software; wrong onboard clock; fixed
onboard computer software.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- H-5
\hazard{H-5}{Someone other than the operator commands the System.}{CATASTROPHIC}{CONTROL}
\field{When} During a contact, or at any time for a source on the spacecraft.
\fieldin{Effect} Another party can do anything the operator can, and hide it,
for the rest of the mission.
\field{Causes} (a)~a command not from the operator arrives (Scenario-300)
[1,~p.~67]; (b)~the means of proving origin is disclosed; (c)~someone else uses
the operator interface, and no second person checks a command (A-4); (d)~a
command arrives from an unexpected system on the spacecraft [1,~p.~62].
\field{Controls} (1)~every command is proven to come from the operator; any
other is refused and logged (O-2, Scenario-300)\ver{TEST}; (2)~only the
operator can use the operator interface\ver{DEMONSTRATION}.
\field{Fails if} The means of proof is disclosed.
\fieldin{Common causes} One proof of origin.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- H-6
\hazard{H-6}{A command is acted on in a form or at a time the operator did not intend.}{CRITICAL}{CAUSE, CONTROL}
\field{When} While a command is checked, held or run.
\fieldin{Effect} The controller does what the operator did not intend; the
ground learns of it at the next contact.
\field{Causes} A command (a)~changed in transfer [1,~p.~62]; (b)~sent again
from an old copy (Scenario-300); (c)~run twice after a lost answer
(Scenario-350); (d)~not allowed in the current mode (Scenario-310) [1,~p.~63];
(e)~with impossible values (Scenario-320) [1,~p.~62]; (f)~dangerous, without
a valid arm (Scenario-330, Scenario-335) [1,~p.~63]; (g)~past its expiry
(Scenario-340) [1,~p.~63]; (h)~scheduled, run at the wrong time or again after
a restart [1,~p.~64]; (i)~held, or the arm state, changed by a bit error
[1,~p.~63]; (j)~built wrongly by the ground software or entered wrongly by the
operator (A-4) [1,~p.~63].
\field{Controls} (1)~the onboard computer checks that every command is new,
allowed in the mode, has possible values and has not expired
(Scenario-120)\ver{TEST}; (2)~a dangerous command needs an arm, which expires
(Scenario-330, Scenario-335)\ver{TEST}; (3)~a repeated command is answered
``already done'' (Scenario-350)\ver{TEST}; (4)~the ground software checks every
command before it is sent (Scenario-120)\ver{TEST}; (5)~every command and
answer is logged (Scenario-120)\ver{INSPECTION}.
\field{Fails if} The checks use a wrong clock; a held command or the arm
state changes after its check.
\fieldin{Common causes} Wrong onboard clock; radiation; data kept over
restarts.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- H-7
\hazard{H-7}{Wrong firmware runs on the controller: damaged, out of date, or with a hidden defect.}{CRITICAL}{CAUSE, CONTROL}
\field{When} During and after an update, a fall-back or a restore.
\fieldin{Effect} The controller misbehaves until a further update. If the
firmware passes its trial, it becomes the known-good firmware the controller
falls back to. If it stops updates, H-3 follows.
\field{Causes} (a)~a hidden defect the trial does not exercise; (b)~firmware
changed after its check, in transfer or in storage [1,~p.~63]; (c)~firmware
older than the minimum version sent again (Scenario-420); (d)~the wrong build,
or wrong configuration values, released [1,~p.~67]; (e)~firmware built for
another controller (Scenario-360); (f)~a defect added by the build tools;
(g)~code released without full testing [1,~p.~67].
\field{Controls} (1)~firmware not judged good within the trial time is given
up (Scenario-430 to Scenario-450)\ver{TEST}; (2)~the onboard computer refuses
firmware that is damaged (Scenario-400), older than the minimum version
(Scenario-420) or built for another controller (Scenario-360)\ver{TEST};
(3)~the controller checks firmware again before it runs it
(Scenario-610)\ver{TEST}; (4)~the ground software checks every release before
it is sent (Scenario-100)\ver{TEST}; (5)~the operator can order a return to
the previous firmware\ver{DEMONSTRATION}.
\field{Fails if} The judgement checks too little, or the trial ends before the
defect shows; a check is made before the last transfer.
\fieldin{Common causes} One set of build tools; radiation.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- H-8
\hazard{H-8}{The controller stops working between contacts and is not brought back.}{CRITICAL}{CAUSE, CONTROL, MONITOR}
\field{When} In any controller mode.
\fieldin{Effect} The controller's work stops until the ground acts at the next
contact.
\field{Causes} (a)~a bit error in running memory (Scenario-620) [1,~p.~63];
(b)~the firmware hangs, deadlocks or overloads [1,~p.~63]; (c)~a restart does
not resume [1,~p.~63]; (d)~the restart takes longer than the time to the next
failure [1,~p.~64]; (e)~the controller misbehaves when no command arrives for a
long time [1,~p.~65]; (f)~the onboard computer misses the silence, or its
watching program has crashed (Scenario-520, Scenario-530).
\field{Controls} (1)~the controller restarts on its own and resumes
(Scenario-620)\ver{TEST}; (2)~the onboard computer notices the silence, tries
to bring the controller back and records it (Scenario-520)\ver{TEST}; (3)~a
crashed onboard computer program restarts and resumes
(Scenario-530)\ver{TEST}; (4)~new firmware that hangs is given up
(Scenario-430)\ver{TEST}.
\field{Fails if} The controller's restart and the onboard computer's watch
fail from one fault.
\fieldin{Common causes} Radiation; data kept over restarts; one power supply;
shared image code.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- H-9
\hazard{H-9}{The ground is shown a false state of the System.}{CRITICAL}{CAUSE, CONTROL}
\field{When} At every contact.
\fieldin{Effect} The operator decides on false information; a harmful command
(H-6) or a missed fault (H-10) follows.
\field{Causes} (a)~wrong time-tags [1,~p.~64]; (b)~old reports shown as
current [1,~p.~63]; (c)~a report changed in storage or in transfer
[1,~p.~62]; (d)~``done'' or ``already done'' answered for a command that did
not run; (e)~the ground software stores or shows a report wrongly
[1,~p.~62].
\field{Controls} (1)~the onboard computer time-tags every report
(Scenario-110)\ver{TEST}; (2)~every report is checked for damage on
arrival\ver{TEST}; (3)~the ground software keeps every report received
[5,~Sec.~3.2]\ver{INSPECTION}.
\field{Fails if} The onboard clock is wrong; a report is damaged before its
check.
\fieldin{Common causes} Wrong onboard clock; radiation.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- H-10
\hazard{H-10}{A fault between contacts never reaches the ground.}{CRITICAL}{CAUSE, MONITOR}
\field{When} Between contacts.
\fieldin{Effect} The ground does not know of the fault; hidden faults pile up,
such as a damaged last-resort firmware (H-1).
\field{Causes} (a)~the fault is not detected [1,~p.~66]; (b)~no event report
is made [1,~p.~65]; (c)~the report is lost to full storage, a crash or a power
loss (Scenario-210, Scenario-530); (d)~the ground loses it.
\field{Controls} (1)~event reports are kept when storage is full
(Scenario-210)\ver{TEST}; (2)~no report is lost without the ground being told
(O-5)\ver{TEST}; (3)~stored reports survive a power loss and a
restart\ver{TEST}.
\field{Fails if} Fault detection depends on the part that has failed.
\fieldin{Common causes} One power supply; radiation.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- H-11
\hazard{H-11}{A valid command is not acted on, and the ground is not told.}{CRITICAL}{CAUSE, CONTROL}
\field{When} While a command is checked, held or run.
\fieldin{Effect} The operator believes an action was taken that was not, for
example a cancelled update (Scenario-470).
\field{Causes} (a)~a full buffer drops the command [1,~p.~63]; (b)~a wrong
onboard clock expires it (Scenario-340); (c)~a scheduled command is lost at a
restart (Scenario-130); (d)~data-link traffic blocks it [1,~p.~63]; (e)~its
answer is lost (Scenario-350).
\field{Controls} (1)~every command gets two answers, received and then done
with the result (Scenario-120)\ver{TEST}; (2)~a refusal carries its reason
(Scenario-310 to Scenario-340)\ver{TEST}; (3)~scheduled commands survive a
restart\ver{TEST}.
\field{Fails if} The answers are lost with the command.
\fieldin{Common causes} Wrong onboard clock; one power supply.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- H-12
\hazard{H-12}{A test function acts outside a test.}{CRITICAL}{CAUSE}
\field{When} In operation.
\fieldin{Effect} Power is cut, a data-link connection is broken or the radio
link is degraded in operation; each is a cause of H-1 or H-4.
\field{Causes} (a)~fault-injection functions stay in the operating software
[1,~p.~64]; (b)~a wrong command, or one not from the operator, starts them.
\field{Controls} (1)~test functions serve verification only and are not part
of the operating software (Capability-5)\ver{INSPECTION}.
\field{Fails if} A bench build is released for operation [1,~p.~67].
\fieldin{Common causes} One set of build tools.

\section{Must-Work and Must-Not-Work Functions}
The hazard analysis identifies the functions that must work and those that
must not work [1,~App.~A.1.2, p.~62]. Both lists hold in operation.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Must work:} return to working firmware when new firmware
fails (H-1); restore through the reprogramming link (H-1, H-2); check every
image before it runs (H-1, H-2, H-7); refuse commands and firmware not from the
operator (H-2, H-5); accept the clock correction (H-4); detect and report
faults between contacts (H-8, H-10); keep and send down every event report
(H-10); answer every command (H-11).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Must not work:} write over the previous or last-resort
firmware (H-1); run firmware not from the operator (H-2); run a command
not from the operator (H-5); run a dangerous command without a valid arm, or
any command twice (H-6); confirm firmware not judged good (H-7); inject faults
(H-12).

\section{Safety-Critical Software}
Software is safety-critical if a hazard analysis finds that it meets at least
one of the criteria a to e [1,~Sec.~4.2, p.~20]. Until the architecture divides
each item into components, the whole item is treated as safety-critical
\tbr{safety}.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lll>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X@{}}
\toprule
Software item & Safety-critical & Criteria & Hazards \\
\midrule
Controller firmware & YES & a, b, c, e & H-1 to H-4, H-6 to H-13 \\
Onboard computer software & YES & a, b, c, e & H-1 to H-17 \\
Ground software & YES & a, b & H-2, H-4 to H-7, H-9, H-10, H-15, H-17 \\
Bench test tools & NO & NONE & NONE \\
Firmware build tools & NO & NONE & NONE \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\noindent\textbf{Legend: criteria} [1,~Sec.~4.2, p.~20]. a: causes or
contributes to a system hazardous condition or event. b: controls functions
identified in a system hazard. c: provides mitigation for a system hazardous
condition or event. d: mitigates damage if a hazardous condition or event
occurs. e: detects, reports and takes corrective action if the system reaches
a potentially hazardous state.

\medskip
{\itshape \noindent\textbf{Note.} Bench test tools stay out of operation
(H-12). Firmware build tools never run in operation; a defect they put into
firmware is a common cause (Section~7.0).\par}
